# Nine Innings

## Responsive layout is a core principle

Every layout, type, color, and interaction change has to work from a narrow phone through a wide desktop. A layout that only fits one size is unfinished.

- When you have a team in the open season, that team's name and purse sit on the left of the header. The purse is abbreviated, reputation then bottlecaps, as in 10 REP · 20 BC. When this coach leads a bid, the held amount follows, as in 2 REP 3 BC (4 REP 5 BC in bid). The season name is not in the header. The SEASON tab shows that season's name, and a long name truncates so DASHBOARD and TEAM stay whole. The three main menu tabs are spaced evenly across the row. The seasons list leaves that spot empty. NineInnings sits level with the team name in the top right, and the coach's name sits level with the purse. The baseball diamond is a logo with no control. It is centered on the sign-in screen at 80 percent of the screen width.
- The diamond scoreboard and the outs counter are not on the page. Each roster kid has a position picker at the bottom of the card, kept to the left. The default is BENCH, in grey, until a position is chosen. A chosen position is pink. Hovering that closed list turns it black on pink. Positions already held by another kid are grey in the list and can still be chosen, which swaps the two kids. The batting order is a second picker on that card, to the right of the position, and it stays hidden while that kid is on the bench. Its default is ORDER, in grey. A chosen spot is pink. A spot another kid holds is grey and can still be chosen, which swaps the two kids. A kid on the bench has no spot in the order. A game waits until the nine and the batting order are both filled. The dashboard puts GAMES above the next game. Every clock the coach reads is local 12-hour, with AM or PM. No screen uses a 24-hour clock. THE POOL hides when it has no kids. Starting the draft fills that pool, so there is no call-kids form. RECRUITING hides when the board is empty or this coach has no team in the season. SCHEDULE hides while the season is forming. Until the season is active, the dashboard is the setup list through PLAY BALL: the steps already done, the one next, and the ones still ahead. A step this person can do has a button. A coach with no team names one from that list while the season is forming. The next game, the play cards, and played games stay off the dashboard through drafting and through a finished draft. When the season is active, this coach's open chores sit at the top of the dashboard. Assigning positions is first when the nine is short, and that card opens the roster. Locking the next game is next when that card is still open, and it opens the matchup. A coach who is caught up does not see empty chores. Open bids this coach has entered sit under those chores as BIDS. A bid this coach leads says YOU HAVE THE HIGH BID at the bottom right and has no raise. A bid this coach still has to answer shows RAISE REPUTATION and RAISE BOTTLECAPS. Tryouts this coach has offered sit under that as TRYOUTS and name how many coaches still have to reply. GAMES stays above the next game. Games start on the dashboard, under the next game. PLAY A GAME VS THE NEIGHBORHOOD RABBLE is a link on the right. The label includes the season's practice rate, as in (1 PER DAY). It waits out that practice gap. PLAY A DEV GAME is a link on the right, and it does not wait and does not count as the last practice. Neither starts until nine kids are assigned and the batting order is filled. A closed link says ASSIGN THE NINE FIRST, or ASSIGN THE BATTING ORDER FIRST. A later step removes that dev link. The roster does not start a game. The purse gains the small practice bottlecaps. Wins, losses, and reputation stay put. Practice games stay off the schedule. HISTORY lists the games already played this season, official or practice or dev, and each one opens its report, once this coach has a team in that season. It shows two games, and MORE... on the right loads the rest. The standings sit above HISTORY once the season is active. That table ranks the teams by wins, then reputation, and shows wins, losses, and reputation. Practice and dev games stay out of it. A coach who has not joined does not see that season's games or recruits. With no active season, sign-in stays on the season list. The report's top card is a radio recap: the forecast, the home advantages in one line, the clubhouse items each club packed, the innings that mattered, a weather change in the half it arrived, the clubs' highlights, and the final call. The score for both clubs follows that card with no LINE heading. This coach's club and kids are blue there, the other club and kids are orange, and a zero in that grid is dim. The items that were used follow the score. The matchup stays the place to lock a card. The other side is the neighborhood: unlined kids from this roster, then kids who exist only for that game. Empty spots on the coach's card are not filled with those kids. The clubhouse lists available items on its own tab, and the buy button names the cost in BC and REP. Under AVAILABLE, the shelf says items are bought for and used in the next game only (official or practice). PACKED FOR NEXT GAME shows only when something is packed. A second copy cannot be packed. HOME FIELD is a team subtab. The coach picks one advantage there, and that pick cannot be changed. The other choices hide after it is picked. The page says it cannot be changed, and that this way gives one advantage. A kid may bring one help that kid or that kid's family can give. About one in twenty new kids does. The helps are MOM'S MUFFINS, which lets the club last a little longer, BROTHER'S VAN, which gives home runners a little speed, SISTER'S SEWING, which gives home fielders a little glove, CAREFUL RAKING, which gives home pitching a little command, and MOWED GRASS, which lets the ball run a little farther. The same help does not stack. It still adds when the coach has already picked a park advantage. The help still shows on the kid in the draft, in the pool, on the recruits, and on the roster. A kid who brings a help has a yellow name on those cards and in DETAILS. It stays on for every home game, a neighborhood game included. An away game does not use it, and a game does not spend it. The choice and the kids' advantages freeze when the card locks. The matchup and the report name them as HOME FIELD. The metal bat is not a series. DRAFT is a season subtab in the place of SCHEDULE while that draft is in progress, and SCHEDULE is that subtab otherwise. The pool card itself is the pick. DETAILS sits in its upper right and opens that kid without making the pick. DRAFTED shows a count, and expanding it shows the name and the seven stats, with no flavor text. The purse stays in the header. DETAILS opens on the season card. It lists official days, practice, weeks, draft rounds, and whether you are the commissioner, and it leaves the season name off. Each fact sits with its label on the left of that card. START THE DRAFT, PLAY BALL, and DELETE SEASON sit at the top of that card with no border. It says THE DRAFT IS COMPLETE. When the draft is finished, PLAY BALL runs on its own. If it cannot start, the setup row says why, and the commissioner can press PLAY BALL on DETAILS. The confirm asks the commissioner to type the season name. THE POOL, RECRUITING, SCHEDULE, and DETAILS are subtabs on that page, lined up on the left. SCHEDULE sits to the left of DETAILS. DRAFT replaces SCHEDULE while the draft is in progress. A menu leaves about one line, then a section header. RECRUITING, DETAILS, and HOME FIELD leave that header off. RECRUITING shows the kids who are for hire. Kids with an open bid are listed first, then kids still open to a bid, then tryouts, then a kid this coach passed. The price sits on the bid button, as in BID 5 BC AND 4 REP. The card body leaves that cost off. The stored reputation cost stays the full price. A climbing club sees THIS BREAK IS FOR CLUBS THAT ARE STILL CLIMBING. A kid who likes a club that is not in first says so. An open bid shows BIDDING IS IN PROGRESS at the bottom right of the recruit card. The high bidder has no button on that card. A coach who has never bid can PASS. A coach in the bid who is not leading sees STOP BIDDING. PASS or STOP BIDDING turns that card dark grey, and the bottom right says PASSED. A tryout card is dark blue until this coach passes, and the name line is the kid. TRYOUT and PASS are the actions until this coach offers a tryout, and there is no bid. After that offer, the bottom right of the card says TRYOUT IS WAITING FOR 2 COACHES while other coaches still have to choose. Once every coach has chosen, the kid joins one club that offered a tryout: the underdog, the best, or a random club among them. If every coach passes, the tryout becomes a priced recruit. The next official game keeps the kid on a win or a strong line, and a loss without a strong line returns the kid to the pool and the writeup says they headed home. The roster marks TRYOUT while the tryout is playing and PLAYING FLAT during a covered bid. Releasing that tryout sends the kid back to the recruiting board as a bid. Releasing any other kid sends that kid back to the pool. The first bid holds that coach's price. A later coach who can pay the bid as it stands joins and spends nothing. The bid stays open until every other real coach has passed, and the last coach still in receives the kid when that coach leads. If every coach passes, the kid stays priced and the passes clear. RAISE REPUTATION and RAISE BOTTLECAPS sit on a dashboard bid this coach still has to answer, and each move only that purse. A bid this coach leads says YOU HAVE THE HIGH BID at the bottom right. COVER WITH BOTTLECAPS asks first, spends the reputation the coach has, and adds two bottlecaps for each reputation point still short. A full roster says RELEASE SOMEONE FIRST. A quiet day can add one recruit. When passed-over kids are past two per real team, an older one may leave, and a kid in an open bid stays. The main menu's leftmost tab is DASHBOARD, and that is where the season opens. The coach's next official game in the open season sits at the top of DASHBOARD and names the other team. It reads that game's forecast from the weather desk. On the schedule, each game sits in a card, and the date is not repeated above those cards. On a schedule card and a game card, the viewing coach's team comes first in the vs line when that coach is in the game. Those names stay the same color. Practice and dev games on the dashboard use a dark blue card. Every scheduled game shows the weather desk's forecast. A played game shows the report from the park. The schedule opens the matchup. Your nine and packed items sit in one box. LOCK is the action at the top right of the next scheduled game's card. It stays closed until nine kids are assigned, and the page says ASSIGN THE NINE FIRST. A game after that says LOCK THE NEXT GAME FIRST. The other side sits in a box below, as locked or still open. When the start time passes, that game plays on its own once both nines are assigned. A short side waits, and the other games still play. The matchup then shows the same radio recap, then the inning scores and both clubs' kid lines in grids, and the clubhouse items that were used when any were packed. Team names in the score grid wrap. No screen shows a series of percents. The recap says the forecast once. A stored writeup stays in the database. The opening air locks when the game is played, and it may change once at the top or the bottom of a later inning. Every day and clock uses the coach's own timezone. The recap is the booth calling the game back in order. It starts with the forecast, names the home advantages in one line and the items each club packed, walks the innings that scored or changed, puts a weather break in the half it arrived, names a few sticks, and closes with the final call. A stored writeup stays in the database. The screen builds the recap from the line, the weather, and the packed items. That card is black. This coach's club and kids are blue, and the other club and kids are orange, in the recap and on the score card under it. A zero on that score card is dim. The score card has no LINE heading. A coach who is not in the game sees those names in the same color. A win adds that game's bottlecaps and reputation and a win in the standings. A loss adds a loss and leaves the bottlecaps and reputation. A tie leaves the purse and the standings. Screen titles are not painted in green. A bye is named and is not a game. OPEN A SEASON is in the coach menu and opens in the center popup. With a season open, that season's name is the middle menu tab. It is not in the header, it is not repeated above the subtabs, and the standings have no season-name heading. The first time a coach signs in, the active season opens. The menu is DASHBOARD, the open season's name, and TEAM, spaced evenly across the row. With no season open, that middle tab says SEASON. TEAM opens the roster. ROSTER, HOME FIELD, and CLUBHOUSE are the subtabs on that page. DASHBOARD is the default landing place. The coach's name opens a dropdown under that name with the season list, OPEN A SEASON, and SIGN OUT. An active season shows its name alone. Any other status follows the name on the same line, as in FALL (forming). The open season is marked. The menu is wide enough that a season name stays on one line. Choosing a season opens that season. The dashboard shows only that season. Cards share one shape: a title at the top left and action buttons at the top right, on the same line, then a divider and the detail lines, on the field-green background. A kid card puts DETAILS in that upper right. It opens that kid alone, and the popup draws CONTACT, POWER, EYE, SPEED, GLOVE, ARM, and STAMINA as bars that match the numbers on the card. The popup names the kid MALE or FEMALE, and it names the help when that kid brings one. On the coach's roster, DETAILS has the same position picker as the card. On those bars and on the numbers, 20 is the most red and 80 is the most green. A picker sits at the bottom left of the card. A roster change in progress shows as a toast and leaves the cards where they are. The active tab in a menu strip uses the pink fill. A button or link turns black on that pink when the pointer can hover. A disabled button or select shows the reason on the page, at the bottom right of the card, without hover. A wait cursor is not the reason. Popups use that same card. The row does not scroll. The body under that row holds the rest of the work.
- That body fills the viewport under the header and the tab row and is the only scroll region. The header and the tabs stay put. Do not add a second page scroll. Scrollbars stay hidden.
- Size type and density from the field container. Do not switch the whole layout at one phone breakpoint.
- The header has to fit a narrow phone: the team name, the site name, and the coach name can truncate.
- Touch targets are at least 44px when the pointer is coarse.
- Inputs stay at least 16px so a phone browser does not zoom the page when a field is focused.
- Use `dvh` and safe-area insets.
- Hover styles apply only when the device can hover.
- Field green is `--field`. Fielders are `--player`. Keep the green easy on the eyes, and keep the fielders dark enough to read on the white bases.
