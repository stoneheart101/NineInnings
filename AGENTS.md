# Nine Innings

## Responsive layout is a core principle

Every layout, type, color, and interaction change has to work from a narrow phone through a wide desktop. A layout that only fits one size is unfinished.

- When you have a team in the open season, that team's name, season, and purse sit on the left of the header. The purse is abbreviated, as in 20 BC · 10 REP. The seasons list leaves that spot empty. NineInnings sits level with the team name in the top right, and the coach's name sits level with the season. The baseball diamond is a logo with no control. It is centered on the sign-in screen at 80 percent of the screen width.
- The diamond scoreboard and the outs counter are not on the page. Each roster kid has a position picker at the bottom of the card, kept to the left. The default is BENCH, in grey. A chosen position is pink. Hovering that closed list turns it black on pink. Positions already held by another kid are grey in the list and can still be chosen, which swaps the two kids. The dashboard puts CURRENT above the next game. Played cards on the dashboard use a 12 hour clock. THE POOL hides when it has no kids. While the season is forming, the commissioner still has that tab so they can call kids. When kids are on the recruiting board, the dashboard says so. Games start on the dashboard, under the next game. One card plays the neighborhood rabble and waits out the season's practice gap. The other plays a dev game, which does not wait and does not count as the last practice. A later step removes that dev card. The roster does not start a game. The purse gains the small practice bottlecaps. Wins, losses, and reputation stay put. Practice games stay off the schedule. The dashboard lists every game already played, official or practice or dev, and each one opens its report. A coach who has not joined still sees that season's played games when an active season exists, and no next game. With no active season, sign-in stays on the season list. DETAILS ranks the teams by wins, then reputation, once the season is active. That table shows wins, losses, and reputation. Practice and dev games stay out of it. The report is the line, the items that were used, and the writeup. The matchup stays the place to lock a card. The other side is the neighborhood: unlined kids from this roster, then kids who exist only for that game. The clubhouse lists available items on its own tab, and the buy button names the cost. DRAFT is a season subtab in the place of SCHEDULE while that draft is in progress, and SCHEDULE is that subtab otherwise. DETAILS titles the season, then lists official days, practice, weeks, draft rounds, and whether you are the commissioner. It says THE DRAFT IS COMPLETE. The commissioner presses PLAY BALL on DETAILS to make the season active. DELETE SEASON is on that card. The confirm asks the commissioner to type the season name. SCHEDULE, THE POOL, RECRUITING, and DETAILS are subtabs on that page, lined up on the left. DRAFT replaces SCHEDULE while the draft is in progress. DETAILS, THE POOL, and RECRUITING have extra space under that row. The dashboard has extra space under the main menu. RECRUITING shows the kids who are for hire, with the reputation and bottlecap costs stored on each kid. A tryout card is dark blue and marked TRYOUT. There is no claim button yet. The main menu's leftmost tab is DASHBOARD, and that is where the season opens. The coach's next official game sits at the top of DASHBOARD and names the other team. Within 10 days it lists each weather chance that is at least 10 percent, highest first, and a later game leaves that forecast off. On the schedule, each game sits in a card. Your team is blue. In a matchup, and on the dashboard, your team name stays the same color as the other names. Practice and dev games on the dashboard use a dark blue card. A game within 10 days shows each weather chance that is at least 10 percent, highest first. A later game is listed without that forecast. The schedule opens the matchup. Your nine and packed items sit in one box. LOCK is the action at the top right of the next scheduled game's card. A game after that says LOCK THE NEXT GAME FIRST. The other side sits in a box below, as locked or still open. When the start time passes, that game plays on its own. The matchup then shows the inning scores and each kid's stats in grids, the clubhouse items that were used when any were packed, and a short writeup of the day. Team names in the score grid wrap. That summary leaves off the weather chances. The realized weather stays in the writeup. Every day and clock uses the coach's own timezone. The writeup names each thing that happened, up to four, and closes with two sentences. A game that already has a writeup keeps it. A dev game can be written again while that card is on the dashboard. A win adds that game's bottlecaps and reputation and a win in the standings. A loss adds a loss and leaves the bottlecaps and reputation. A tie leaves the purse and the standings. Screen titles are not painted in green. A bye is named and is not a game. OPEN A SEASON is in the coach menu and opens in the center popup. With a season open, that season's name stays in the header and is the title on DETAILS. It is not repeated above the subtabs. The first time a coach signs in, the active season opens. The menu is DASHBOARD, SEASON, ROSTER, and CLUBHOUSE. DASHBOARD is the default landing place. The coach's name opens a popup in the center of the screen with the rules version, the team list, OPEN A SEASON, and SIGN OUT. Cards share one shape: a title at the top left and action buttons at the top right, on the same line, then a divider and the detail lines, on the field-green background. A picker sits at the bottom left of the card. The active tab in a menu strip uses the pink fill. A button or link turns black on that pink when the pointer can hover. Popups use that same card. The row does not scroll. The body under that row holds the rest of the work.
- That body fills the viewport under the header and the tab row and is the only scroll region. The header and the tabs stay put. Do not add a second page scroll.
- Size type and density from the field container. Do not switch the whole layout at one phone breakpoint.
- The header has to fit a narrow phone: the team name, the site name, and the coach name can truncate.
- Touch targets are at least 44px when the pointer is coarse.
- Inputs stay at least 16px so a phone browser does not zoom the page when a field is focused.
- Use `dvh` and safe-area insets.
- Hover styles apply only when the device can hover.
- Field green is `--field`. Fielders are `--player`. Keep the green easy on the eyes, and keep the fielders dark enough to read on the white bases.
